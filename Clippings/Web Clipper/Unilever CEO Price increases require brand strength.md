![](https://marketingweek.imgix.net/uploads/2025/11/Home-Care-unilever.jpg?auto=compress,format&crop=faces,entropy,edges&fit=crop&q=60&w=1200&h=700) Strengthening brand equity is the only way to justify increasing prices above market levels, says Unilever’s CEO.

Speaking during the consumer goods giant’s third quarter results today (28 October), CEO Fernando Fernandez outlined the company’s “discipline” when it comes to ensuring any price increases are paired with strengthening the brand or driving premium innovation.

“You can only increase pricing relative to market through the strengthening of your brand equities,” Fernandez told investors on the call.

Unilever has been on a turnaround journey over the past few years, which has seen it focus on driving volume growth, rather than relying primarily on pricing to drive sales – as had been the strategy in the years of high inflation post-pandemic.

During the second quarter the Dove owner drove its [best volume growth in 15 years](https://www.marketingweek.com/unilever-volume-growth-stronger-brands/) at 5.5%. This most recent quarter saw volumes grow by 2.9%, with pricing contributing a further 2.3% to total sales growth of 5.2%.

> The strength of our brands, the momentum within our business and the increasing consistency of our execution gives us the confidence.
> 
> ###### Srinivas Phatak, Unilever

With higher cost inflation coming in the quarter, pricing played more of a role for Unilever. However, Fernandez said he was “pleased” with the performance, which saw the company achieve both volume and pricing growth.

“The ambition of growing at least 2% volume growth is absolutely fundamental for us and we will ensure that we have a good balance between volume and pricing in whatever we do in the short and in the medium term,” he stated.

Unilever will seek to pair price increases with meaningful investments in brand equity and innovation, the company stated.

The FMCG giant will “ensure that \[it is\] competitive in both pricing and marketing investment”, said Fernandez, as it looks to drive volume in a near-future where pricing is expected to play more of a role.

The business is facing what chief financial officer Srinivas Phatak called an “uncertain” environment, but Unilever is confident it is well-equipped to achieve success.

“The strength of our brands, the momentum within our business and the increasing consistency of our execution gives us the confidence in delivering our output,” the CFO said.
