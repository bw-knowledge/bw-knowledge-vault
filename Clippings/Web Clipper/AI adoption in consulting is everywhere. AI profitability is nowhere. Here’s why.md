# Full Article

AI adoption in consulting is everywhere. AI profitability is nowhere. Here’s why.  
  
Almost every consulting firm started with the same idea: give everyone AI subscriptions, encourage experimentation, and assume the efficiency gains will eventually hit the bottom line.  
  
Wrong thinking.  
  
Meanwhile, this is what we hear/see:  
- AI and token costs start eating into margins.  
- Consultants produce more drafts, analyses and experiments, including work nobody asked for.  
- The fantasy of eliminating junior consultants runs into the realities of delivery and quality control.  
- The billable hour AI squeeze hits: fewer hours to sell, while clients can do more work themselves.  
- Leaders get nervous, blame users, and ask the CFO to tighten spending.  
  
And the consulting commercial model? Largely untouched.  
  
HubSpot CEO [****Yamini Rangan****](https://www.linkedin.com/in/yaminirangan/) recently put it well: “AI adoption is everywhere. AI outcomes are not.”  
  
Apollo chief economist [****Torsten Slok****](https://www.linkedin.com/in/torsten-slok-8a7520a2/): “Despite massive investments and widespread hype around artificial intelligence, the technology has delivered zero financial payoff to corporate America outside of tech.”  
  
Consulting leaders should pay attention.  
  
A faster consulting team does not automatically mean a more profitable consulting firm. Especially when the firm still sells broad capabilities, reinvents its approach for every client and charges for effort.  
  
The commercial sequence matters:  
  
1. Design propositions around specific, recurring client problems, the outcomes that can be achieved and the credible path to those outcomes.  
2. Turn this proposition into a visible, signature approach with a repeatable client success journey.  
3. Apply AI to that repeatable core, with deliberate workflows, human roles, quality controls and cost discipline.  
4. Capture the gains through stronger client attraction, pricing power, gross margins and an increasing revenue per FTE trend.  
  
This applies to boutique consulting firms and to large-firm practices, sectors, and service lines inside larger firms.  
  
👉 𝗧𝗛𝗘 𝗧𝗔𝗞𝗘𝗔𝗪𝗔𝗬  
  
The commercial sequence above shows how consulting firms progressively shift revenue from selling capacity to monetising repeatable expertise.  
  
Giving the whole team AI access and telling them to experiment can help them learn. As an ROI strategy, it’s a gamble. It can even make the firm less productive.  
  
The real test: Is AI helping the consulting business attract better work, deliver it more profitably and grow revenue per FTE?  
  
Otherwise, the clearest AI ROI may sit on the software vendor’s P&L.
