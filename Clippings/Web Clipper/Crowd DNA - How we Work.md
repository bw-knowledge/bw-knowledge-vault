In summary, Crowd DNA argues that brands win commercially by tapping into slow, deep-rooted culture rather than chasing fast-moving trends, and sets out a three-step method (decode, reframe, act) delivered by blended "Cultural Strategist" teams.

## Headlines

- **Culture is getting messier, and the mess is the opportunity.** Society is polarised, atomised and fragmented, people hold contradictory identities, and consumer journeys are non-linear. The guide positions untangling that complexity, rather than avoiding it, as the core job of cultural strategy.
- **The bigger wins come from slow culture, not fast culture.** Slow culture covers enduring values, traditions and practices, while fast culture covers rapid trend-driven shifts shaped by technology and media. The guide says brands that only react to fast culture miss as much, if not more, opportunity than those who tap the slow.
- **The method is research, analysis and strategy: decode, reframe, act.** Research collects stories from people, data, and codes and signals, and is deliberately method-agnostic (ethnography, quantitative, semiotics). Analysis finds the tensions beneath those stories, and strategy turns them into audience, brand, category, product and channel opportunities.
- **Named frameworks structure the work.** These include Cultural Strategy Layers (fast to slow culture, from signals up to human needs), an ethnographic observation framework, the "altitude of tension", a three-step cultural-orthodoxy-to-ideological-opportunity model, and a "culturally authentic space" found where culture (relevance), category (differentiation) and company (credibility) overlap.
- **The guide argues for people-first research and blended teams.** It rejects starting from "consumers" and shopping baskets in favour of the human experience, and favours casting participants over recruiting to panels. Teams combine semioticians, ethnographers and qualitative and quantitative researchers under the single title of Cultural Strategist, and AI is used only where it fixes or improves something.
