In summary, the FCA has established regulatory expectations for firms and technology providers developing agentic AI products in financial services, clarifying that responsibility for compliance remains with regulated firms regardless of whether systems are developed in-house or by third parties, and warning that the boundary between regulated and unregulated activity will be enforced.

## Headlines

- **The FCA expects regulated firms to retain full compliance responsibility for agentic AI systems regardless of their origin.** Senior managers must demonstrate understanding of system autonomy, risks, and real-time controls, with particular attention to how AI agents interact with customers and handle transactions.

- **Technology providers and social media platforms must determine whether agentic AI products constitute regulated financial services before launch.** The FCA emphasizes that describing a business as a "technology provider" does not exempt it from authorization requirements if it carries out activities such as payments services, credit broking, or financial advice.

- **Agentic AI products blur boundaries between regulated and unregulated activity, requiring firms to establish clear governance for agent-initiated transactions and customer support.** Firms must consider whether to accept payments instructed by AI agents, establish appropriate consents, and define what actions agents are permitted to undertake.

- **The FCA will intervene against unauthorized regulated financial services offered through agentic products and has launched an Agentic Policy Sprint to facilitate cross-industry collaboration.** The regulator emphasizes that AI providers must include clear disclaimers about the limits of AI-generated financial information alongside effective operational safeguards.

# Our approach to Agentic AI

First published: 24/11/2026Last updated: 24/11/2026

Our expectations for firms, technology providers and social media platforms looking to develop and deploy agentic AI products in the financial services sector.

## On this page ![](https://www.fca.org.uk/themes/custom/fca/images/document-blue.svg)

- [Update on 24 November 2026](https://www.fca.org.uk/firms/innovation/ai-approach/agentic-ai#section-update-on-24-november-2026)
- [Information for regulated firms](https://www.fca.org.uk/firms/innovation/ai-approach/agentic-ai#section-information-for-regulated-firms)
- [Information for technology providers or social media platforms](https://www.fca.org.uk/firms/innovation/ai-approach/agentic-ai#section-information-for-technology-providers-or-social-media-platforms)

We recently published a [statement (PDF)Link is external](https://www.drcf.org.uk/siteassets/drcf/pdf-files/drcf-joint-statement-protecting-people-and-businesses-in-and-agentic-economy2.pdf?v=427414) along with regulators that are part of the [Digital Regulation Cooperation Forum (DRCF)](https://www.fca.org.uk/about/how-we-operate/who-work-with/drcf "Digital Regulation Cooperation Forum") setting out our views on firms developing and deploying personal AI agents.

We are now setting out more specific expectations for those looking to develop and deploy products in the financial services sector, some of which may require FCA regulation at launch or evolve in a way that makes regulation apply.

To support the development and roll out of agentic products at pace, we will provide regular updates on this webpage. This information is relevant to regulated firms, and those who are not regulated but considering developing agentic AI products.

## Update on 24 November 2026

Across financial services, firms are moving from experimentation to deployment of AI agents.  We are engaging with firms looking to offer personal AI agent products to UK consumers.

AI is redefining financial services. Deployed well, AI can increase productivity, improve competition, services and drive economic growth. AI agents can widen access to financial tools and information and be part of new products that offer significant consumer benefit and convenience. But these products can also blur the boundary between regulated and unregulated activity, and the protections consumers receive in relation to their finances and data.

We and other regulators welcome early engagement and provide services to support those looking to innovate so they’re clear on what regulatory requirements may apply and to test products.

As AI becomes capable of moving beyond providing information to initiating transactions, moving money, arranging borrowing or making investment decisions, all at speed and scale, firms must understand which activities require authorisation and where regulatory requirements may apply. They must be mindful of the operational resilience of their platforms and their commercial partners.

We have powers to intervene if we believe regulated financial services are being offered without our authorisation and will not hesitate to use them if necessary. They’re in place to provide appropriate safeguards against scams and financial loss, and ensure consumers have effective support and redress when things go wrong.

We will assess the services firms provide through agentic products, including how they develop over time, to ensure they keep to regulatory requirements.

We expect AI providers to help consumers understand the limits of financial information and advice generated by their models through clear, proportionate and consistent disclaimers and also bolster these with effective operational safeguards.

Our [AI Lab](https://www.fca.org.uk/firms/innovation/ai-lab "AI Lab") provides a range of services to support firms develop and test new agentic services and products. We intend to facilitate cross-industry engagement through the launch of a dedicated Agentic Policy Sprint for firms, policymakers and tech providers to collaborate and unlock innovation in a safe and responsible way. You can register expressions of interest by emailing [AgenticAI@fca.org.uk](mailto:AgenticAI@fca.org.uk).

## Information for regulated firms

### Developing, deploying or relying on agentic AI

If your regulated firm develops or deploys agentic AI or relies on a third-party provider, responsibility for complying with regulatory obligations remains with you and senior managers within your firm. Our regulatory framework is principles-based and outcomes-focused and applies when regulated firms use AI.

We expect you to demonstrate that you understand the systems you use, the degree of autonomy those systems have, the risks they create and the controls necessary to manage those risks in real time and in stress situations.

Our [AI good practice and areas for improvement](https://www.fca.org.uk/publications/good-poor-practice/ai-financial-services "AI in financial services: good practice and areas for improvement") sets out areas for you to consider and highlights practical examples of how firms have done this for AI use cases.

Under the [Consumer Duty](https://www.fca.org.uk/firms/consumer-duty "Consumer Duty"), firms' obligations include avoiding causing foreseeable harm, ensuring that consumers get the information they need and helping support customers to pursue their financial objectives.

The Duty also contains specific provisions on governance and culture, monitoring consumer outcomes and redress or other appropriate action.

Senior managers are responsible for any AI models deployed within the area of the business they are responsible for, whether these are developed in house or by a third party.

They need to take reasonable steps to understand the risks arising from AI, provide effective oversight, challenge deployment and make sure governance, controls and escalation arrangements are appropriate for the risks.

You should also consider how you will respond when customers use AI agents to interact with you, including how you establish that you are dealing with an agent, what consents are in place, what an AI agent is allowed to do, and how customers can get support if something goes wrong.  This also includes information provided to consumers through agentic chatbots or where agents are used to help resolve complaints.

You should consider your systems and controls, any liabilities that may arise from accepting instructions and consider whether it is appropriate to accept payments instructed by an AI agent, or from a digital wallet controlled by an AI agent.

## Information for technology providers or social media platforms

### Understand the activities your agents undertake

If you are a technology provider or social media platform considering launching an agentic product in the UK, you need to consider whether you are carrying out activities regulated by the FCA – so [speak to us](https://www.fca.org.uk/firms/innovation/our-innovation-services "Our innovation services").

Technology providers should consider carefully the activities their agents actually undertake. Just because a business describes itself as a technology provider does not mean that they do not need to be authorised by us to provide financial services in the UK. This boundary is set by Parliament.

You need to think about whether you are carrying out regulated activities or if other regulatory requirements apply.

This could, for example, include consideration about whether you are offering payments services, issuing financial promotions, providing an authorised information service, engaging in credit broking or intermediation or facilitating or offering financial advice.

We recognise these questions can be complex. Personalised context, which some large language models rely on, can amplify this complexity. If your company is developing or deploying agentic AI products, you can engage with us through our [innovation services](https://www.fca.org.uk/firms/innovation/our-innovation-services "Our innovation services") and [pre-application support service](https://www.fca.org.uk/firms/authorisation/pre-application-support-service "Pre-application support service (PASS)").
