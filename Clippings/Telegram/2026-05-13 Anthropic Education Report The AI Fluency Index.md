In summary, users who engage in iterative refinement with AI develop significantly stronger overall AI fluency behaviors, but paradoxically become less critical when AI produces polished-looking outputs like code or documents.

## Headlines

- **Iteration and refinement is the strongest predictor of AI fluency.** Conversations exhibiting iteration show 2.67 fluency behaviors on average compared to 1.33 in non-iterative conversations, with users 5.6x more likely to question reasoning and 4x more likely to identify missing context.

- **Users become less evaluative when AI generates artifacts.** Despite being more directive upfront when creating code, documents, or apps, users are 3.1–5.2 percentage points less likely to question reasoning or identify missing context in artifact conversations.

- **The 4D AI Fluency Framework identifies 24 specific behaviors for safe human-AI collaboration.** Only 11 are directly observable in Claude.ai conversations; the other 13 (including honest disclosure and consequence consideration) occur outside the interface and require qualitative assessment.

- **Early adopters show consistent fluency patterns across languages and weekdays.** Analysis of 9,830 Claude.ai conversations from January 2026 revealed stable behavioral indicators across six languages and all days of the week, suggesting habits rather than artifacts of timing or culture.

- **Critical evaluation of polished outputs represents an emerging skill gap.** As AI models produce increasingly functional-looking results, the ability to critically assess accuracy and completeness becomes more valuable but is practiced less frequently.
