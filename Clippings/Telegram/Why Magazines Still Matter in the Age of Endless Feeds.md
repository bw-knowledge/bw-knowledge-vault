In summary, magazines have evolved from mass-market publishing into niche cultural products that provide editorial intentionality, permanence, and tangible presence in an attention economy dominated by ephemeral digital feeds.

## Headlines

- **Print magazines function as curated taste markers rather than mass-reach media.** Titles like Apartamento, Holiday, and The Gentlewoman demonstrate that magazines can maintain cultural influence without relying on broad circulation numbers, instead serving as markers of identity and perspective.

- **Major media conglomerates now treat magazines as one layer within diversified editorial ecosystems.** Hearst generates substantial profits from B2B products, Condé Nast positions print within broader experiences and partnerships, and Monocle integrates magazines with radio, retail, cafés, events, and creative services under a unified editorial worldview.

- **Magazines offer permanence and hierarchy that social media feeds cannot provide.** Unlike digital content that disappears into endless timelines, printed magazines materialize editorial choices into tangible objects with weight, texture, and duration, forcing brands to edit deliberately and sustain ideas longer.

- **Print restores cognitive processing time lost to algorithmic feeds.** Social media overwhelms the brain with rapid context-switching between unrelated content, while magazines allow ideas to settle through deliberate editorial sequencing and visual hierarchy.

This year marks 30 years since I first started working in journalism, much of it spent in print media. And perhaps the most honest thing I can say about the industry is this: print really did lose the economic power, scale and cultural centrality it once had.

Circulations shrank. Advertising moved elsewhere. Newsstands disappeared from many cities. The industrial logic that sustained monthly magazines for decades has been under pressure for quite some time.

But perhaps the more interesting story begins there.

Over the past few months, I’ve been — once again — researching the global magazine market more deeply and, above all, the business ecosystems behind the brands that still publish today, whether they are traditional media companies or not.

And the picture is far less binary than the usual “print is dead” narrative. Many of the most resilient media brands no longer rely on magazines alone.

Hearst — once synonymous with consumer magazines — now generates a substantial part of its profits from B2B products and services. Condé Nast increasingly treats print as one layer within a broader ecosystem of experiences, partnerships and cultural positioning. And [Monocle](https://uk.linkedin.com/company/monocle?trk=article-ssr-frontend-pulse_little-mention) is perhaps the clearest example of this model: magazine, radio, newsletters, books, retail, cafés, events, travel guides, brand partnerships and [Winkreative](https://uk.linkedin.com/company/winkreative?trk=article-ssr-frontend-pulse_little-mention), its creative agency, all working as parts of the same editorial worldview.

Titles such as Apartamento, Holiday and The Gentlewoman also show that magazines can still function as influential cultural products without necessarily depending on mass circulation. The magazine has become less of a broad-reach medium and more of a marker of taste, identity and perspective.

A few days ago, in a post I read here by [Débora Emm](https://br.linkedin.com/in/d%C3%A9bora-emm-a911857b?trk=article-ssr-frontend-pulse_little-mention), I came across a [Talk Inc. Research](https://br.linkedin.com/company/talk-inc-research?trk=article-ssr-frontend-pulse_little-mention) study presented by [Carla Mayumi Albertuni](https://br.linkedin.com/in/cmayumi/en?trk=article-ssr-frontend-pulse_little-mention). In the discussion that followed, [Rosana Hermann](https://br.linkedin.com/in/rosanahermann?trk=article-ssr-frontend-pulse_little-mention) made a point that stayed with me: on social media, our brains barely have time to process one subject before another immediately takes its place. A war. An advert. A death. A recipe. A meme.

In the end, very little settles.

Perhaps part of the value of print today lies precisely in restoring hierarchy, context, intention and presence.

At the launch of the latest issue of [The Summer Hunter](https://br.linkedin.com/company/the-summer-hunter?trk=article-ssr-frontend-pulse_little-mention) magazine — yes, we do publish a yearly, bold, glossy, bookish mag! — we hosted a conversation at Megafauna bookshop in São Paulo, brilliantly moderated by [Cristina Naumovs](https://br.linkedin.com/in/cristinanaumovs?trk=article-ssr-frontend-pulse_little-mention), about the place of print today.

At one point, my friend [Oga Mendonça](https://br.linkedin.com/in/oga-mendon%C3%A7a-3374a580?trk=article-ssr-frontend-pulse_little-mention) said something that stayed with me: magazines materialise the present.

That struck me as an unusually precise way of putting it.

Print turns an editorial choice, an image, a conversation or a cultural obsession into something that physically exists in the world. It has weight, texture, format, a beginning and an end.

Digital content passes by. You see it, perhaps save it, perhaps even mean to return to it — and then it disappears into the endless flow of the timeline. A magazine doesn’t. It stays put. Not as nostalgia, but as presence.

What interests me about print isn’t nostalgia. It’s its ability to synthesise ideas, aesthetics and behaviours into something tangible. A printed magazine forces a brand to edit more carefully, choose more deliberately and sustain an idea for longer.

It demands permanence in a world increasingly designed around disappearance.

So this is what I wanted to say: from now on, I’ll be coming back here, from time to time, to think out loud about print — magazines, newspapers, paper, publishing, permanence and the business models that still make all of this possible.

Not necessarily about books or magazines alone.

But about the things that should probably continue to exist, even when the lights go out.

The image illustrating this post belongs to [Casa Magazines \| Global News Inc.](https://www.linkedin.com/company/casa-magazines-nyc/?trk=article-ssr-frontend-pulse_little-text-block) in NYC, one of Manhattan’s largest and most important magazine shops, still carrying some of the world’s most interesting print titles.
