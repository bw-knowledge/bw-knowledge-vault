## Headlines

- **Search engines are still the default gateway — nearly nine in ten searchers used one in the last 30 days.** In Great Britain, 85% of online searchers used a search engine in the past 30 days, and it remains the leading *starting point* across every information task tested, from asking questions (67%) to buying products (60%).

- **AI assistants have entered the journey but aren't the mainstream daily habit yet.** 58% of online searchers at least sometimes start a query with an AI assistant, but only 18% do so daily ("frequent" searchers) and 35% never start with AI. Britain sits near the bottom of a 19-market comparison at 54% AI searchers — ahead of only the U.S. (48%) and far behind India, Indonesia and the UAE (89%).

- **Growth is concentrated among existing users, not new converts.** 71% of current AI searchers say they use AI for search more than a year ago (31% "much more"); 46% expect to use it more next year, rising to 62% of frequent users. Among non-users, only 12% expect to increase use and 59% don't expect to start at all.

- **The leading AI use cases are answer-and-resolve tasks, not link-finding.** Among AI searchers, the top uses are getting a direct answer (54%), summarising (34%), verifying information (28%), comparing options (25%) and troubleshooting (24%) — all tasks where people want the work of search reduced, not just a list of links.

- **Search engines hold a decisive trust advantage; AI assistants trail most sources.** Only 31% of online searchers trust information from AI assistants — above social media (17%) but below video platforms (34%), forums (39%), search engines (77%) and maps (83%). Trust in AI jumps to 49% among people who actually use it.

- **A new digital divide is opening between AI users and non-users.** AI searchers outstrip non-users on every dimension: expecting to increase usage (46% vs 12%), trusting AI results (49% vs 8%) and responding to at least one trust signal (87% vs 45%). 37% of non-users say *no* feature would increase their trust, suggesting transparency tooling alone won't convert them.

- **Younger adults treat more platforms as search tools.** Gen Z and Millennials pull in video, social, forums and AI far more than older adults: Millennials lead on AI assistants (42%), Gen Z leads on social media (49%) and online communities (37%). Search is mobile-first overall (60%), rising to 71% among Gen Z.

### Foreword

The search box is one of the internet's most familiar interfaces: users type a query, search engines return links, and people decide which sources to trust. AI assistants challenge that pattern by offering an answer itself rather than a list of links. YouGov's findings suggest Brits are **not** abandoning search engines, but AI assistants are carving out a role in the search journey — especially when users want answers to direct questions, summaries, or help checking information.

The central challenge is trust. AI assistants may be useful because they reduce the work of search, but consumers still want to verify what they find, relying on multiple sources, official websites and source links to judge credibility. That makes the future of search "less a battle between links and answers than a race to deliver both: fast, useful answers backed by visible proof." — *Mimi Smith-Jones, Research Manager, YouGov*

### Report takeaways

- Traditional search engines remain the default gateway. Nearly nine in ten online searchers (85%) used a search engine in the past 30 days, and search engines are the leading starting point across the information tasks examined.
- Gen Z and Millennials use a wider set of platforms. Video, forums, social media and AI assistants all play a larger role in younger adults' search behaviour.
- Current AI searchers are more likely to say their use has increased than decreased. Expected growth is concentrated among people who already use AI regularly — pointing to deeper engagement rather than broad non-user conversion.
- Leading AI use cases are direct answers, summaries and verification, with comparison and troubleshooting also prominent.
- Search engines have a clear trust advantage. But the bigger divide is between current AI searchers (open to AI answers, source links and personalisation controls) and non-users, many of whom remain hard to persuade.

### The online search landscape in 2026

- **Daily information-seeking is common.** 65% of Brits look up information online every day. More than a third of Gen Z (43%) and Millennials (40%) do so six or more times daily, declining among Gen X (34%) and Baby Boomers+ (16%). High-income earners are much more likely to search 6+ times a day (45%).
- **Search engines are the dominant channel.** In the past 30 days: search engine 85%, maps/navigation 39%, news site/app 39%, social media 33%, video platform 32%, AI assistant 31%, marketplace 21%, online forum 20%.
- **Younger adults treat more platforms as search tools.** Search engines lead in every generation, but Millennials use AI assistants most (42%), while Gen Z leads on social media (49%) and online communities (37%). Usage of these channels drops sharply among Gen X and Baby Boomers.

### AI assistants enter the search journey

- **Regular for some, absent for many.** 58% of online searchers at least sometimes start with an AI assistant ("AI searchers"); 35% never do ("non-AI searchers"). Frequency across all searchers: frequent (≥1/day) 18%, regular (several/week) 16%, occasional (≤1/week) 24%, none 35%, don't know 8%. Gen Z leads daily adoption at 25%.
- **Britain is a comparatively low-adoption market.** GB sits near the bottom of the 19-market ranking at 54% AI searchers — ahead only of the U.S. (48%) and well behind India, Indonesia and the UAE (89%), Saudi Arabia (86%), Singapore and Switzerland (82%).
- **Usage is rising, mostly among existing users.** 71% of AI searchers use AI in search more than a year ago (31% "much more"); only 5% use it less. Among regular users, 54% use it "much more." Looking ahead, 46% of AI searchers expect to use it more next year (62% of frequent users), versus just 12% of non-users — and 59% of non-users don't expect to start at all.
- **Leading use cases (AI searchers):** direct answer 54%, summarising 34%, verifying information 28%, comparing options 25%, troubleshooting 24%, planning/recommendations 22%, deciding what to search next 12%.
- **AI is part of a broader journey, not the whole of it.** Only 15% of AI searchers call AI their *starting point*; most use it alongside other sources (36%), after other searches (21%), or occasionally for specific questions (24%).
- **After an AI answer:** 22% click through to links, 22% compare with other apps/sites, 24% stop searching, 20% "it depends on the topic", 7% search other topics. Among frequent AI searchers, clicking AI-supplied links (29%) is more common than stopping at the answer (24%).
- **Profile of frequent AI searchers:** 18% of online searchers; skew male (62%); more likely Gen Z (22% vs 15% nat rep) and working full-time (50% vs 42%); more likely to click through AI-supplied links (29% vs 22% of AI searchers) and to verify by asking a follow-up question (41% vs 24%).

### The trust gap: AI assistants vs. search engines

- **AI assistants trail most sources on trust.** Share who trust each source ("a lot" + "somewhat"): maps/navigation 83%, search engine 77%, news site/app 61%, other info sites 44%, marketplace 42%, online community/forum 39%, video platform 34%, AI assistant 31%, social media 17%. AI trust rises to 49% among people who use it (and is 32% among Gen Z).
- **No single trust signal moves non-users.** For AI searchers, the top single signal that would increase trust is the answer coming from an official source (19%), followed by a clear indication of how the AI arrived at its answer (18%) and clear links to sources (16%). Among non-AI searchers, 37% say *none* of these would sway them.
- **Personalisation faces a privacy ceiling.** 62% of non-AI searchers are not comfortable with their data being used for tailored answers; AI searchers are more open, with 32% comfortable *if* they can control or turn it off.
- **A new digital divide (AI searchers vs non-AI searchers):** will increase AI usage 46% vs 12%; trust AI results 49% vs 8%; responsive to at least one trust signal 87% vs 45%; not comfortable with personalisation 27% vs 62%. AI searchers are more open on every measure; non-users are much less likely to expect to start and more likely to say trust features wouldn't change their view.

### Methodology

YouGov Surveys: Serviced. Multi-region online survey conducted 21 April – 29 May 2026. Great Britain n=2,003 (nationally representative, adults 18+); 19 markets total (n≈1,000 each, U.S. n=1,494, Hong Kong n=508). Online searchers (n>1,800): look for information online at least once a week. AI searchers (n>1,000): start new queries with an AI assistant — frequent (n>300, ≥1/day), regular (n>250, several/week), occasional (n>400, ≤1/week); non-AI searchers (n>800) selected "None"/"Don't know". Generations: Gen Z (1997+), Millennial (1981–1996), Gen X (1965–1980), Baby Boomers+ (pre-1964).
