# The rise of Palantir: pro-West, ‘anti-woke’ software giant

## As London’s mayor blocks the company’s contract with the Metropolitan Police, boss Alex Karp claims its AI is an engine for western victory

There are few places where Alex Karp is truly comfortable. The billionaire chief of Palantir is a self-proclaimed introvert — a singleton who is “happily not married, not divorced” and resides in rural New Hampshire, miles from his nearest neighbour.

Yet one place he does feel at home is as a societal pariah. “I’m viewed as the batshit-crazy guy who is often telling you something you don’t want to hear and you may not like, but is probably right,” he said at a recent conference.

And one thing of which he is certain? Palantir, the data analytics giant that fancies itself the “operating system” of western intelligence, military and law enforcement, is the pariah we all need. It goes places and does things that most other tech companies don’t — and won’t.

![Defence Secretary John Healey and Palantir Technologies CEO Alex Karp sign a £1.5 billion investment agreement.](https://www.thetimes.com/imageserver/image/d10f85ba-f41d-401c-bb7c-0c4ffc3e309e.jpg?strip=all&format=webp&resize=2360)John Healey, the defence secretary, and Palantir’s chief executive Alex Karp sign an investment deal during President Trump’s state visit in September last year Lucy North/PA

It powered Israel’s war machine as Gaza was pulverised. Its software has been used to track down migrants for Donald Trump’s controversial Immigration and Customs Enforcement (ICE) department. Clients include the Pentagon, the [Ministry of Defence](https://www.thetimes.com/uk/defence/article/palantir-ministry-of-defence-mod-wglwx6rvl) and any other intelligence agency in the West, which Karp sees as caught in a new civilisational fight against China for supremacy in artificial intelligence.

- [**Louis Mosley: Palantir helps keep Londoners safe. That’s what Sadiq Khan should value**](https://www.thetimes.com/business/companies-markets/article/louis-mosley-palantir-met-sadiq-khan-london-3mc2k6tbf)

The atomic age, in his view, is ending. AI is the new deterrent, and Karp sees Palantir as an engine for western victory. “We are here to disrupt and make the institutions we partner with the very best in the world, and, when it’s necessary, to scare enemies and on occasion kill them.”

It was against this backdrop that [London’s mayor, Sir Sadiq Khan](https://www.thetimes.com/comment/the-times-view/article/sadiq-khan-metropolitan-police-palantir-2ksx5rpxp), last week stirred controversy by blocking a £50 million contract that the Metropolitan Police was set to award Palantir to automate intelligence gathering for investigations. The decision was due, the mayor’s office said, to a breach of procurement rules: Palantir was the only company considered for the contract.

![London Mayor Sadiq Khan speaking with two Metropolitan Police officers.](https://www.thetimes.com/imageserver/image/0a1e8c39-4338-4e47-9919-2ebda31e32a6.jpg?strip=all&format=webp&resize=2360)Sir Sadiq KhanYui Mok/PA

Allies of the mayor suggested the real reason may have been different. “There is a wider question … about whether the values and ethics of a company should also be taken into account,” one said.

Peter Kyle, the business secretary, refused to throw his support behind his fellow Labour politician. “Sadiq has to explain himself. I’m not here to explain for him. I’m the first to admit that some of the work that Palantir does, no other country in the world, no other business in the world, company in the world, organisation in the world, is capable of doing,” he said.

![Peter Kyle, Secretary of State for Business and Trade, at the Emma Bridgewater pottery factory.](https://www.thetimes.com/imageserver/image/9a1e530a-8456-41d1-ba5a-99b50b0a9f31.jpg?strip=all&format=webp&resize=2360)Peter KylePAUL TONGE FOR THE SUNDAY TIMES

It is a capability born of decades of toil in the shadow of public opprobrium; Karp relocated the company to Colorado in 2020 to escape regular protests at its Palo Alto headquarters. But in the past couple of years, the 58-year-old has shifted from righteous defensiveness to aggressive triumphalism as conflicts in the Middle East and Ukraine have led to a surge in demand for Palantir’s products.

Advertisement

The company’s profits hit $753 million (£562 million) in [the first three months of the year](https://www.thetimes.com/business/technology/article/palantir-increases-revenue-forecast-after-software-sales-double-7vvzzwv20) — a fourfold increase on the $176 million that it earned in the same period last year. Sales nearly doubled to $1.6 billion. Palantir’s shares closed on Friday at $137, a 20-fold increase since 2023, valuing the company at $328 billion.

“We are doing things unlike any other company has done, which of course has been confounding to people,” Karp told analysts recently. And for the sceptics? “I love the idea of getting a drone and having light fentanyl-laced urine spraying on analysts that tried to screw us.”

## The start of Palantir

[Peter Thiel](https://www.thetimes.com/business/technology/article/palantir-founder-peter-thiel-antichrist-lectures-religion-qzmpth35t) needed a chief executive. It was 2003 and the arch libertarian, known as the investor who wrote Mark Zuckerberg his first cheque, had just sold PayPal to eBay. He had an idea. One of the core failings that had led to the 9/11 attacks was that American and other western agencies had not knitted together intelligence that might have helped avert the tragedy. Thiel thought that PayPal’s anti-fraud algorithms could be repurposed to fight terrorism.

![Peter Thiel speaking at a news conference in Tokyo.](https://www.thetimes.com/imageserver/image/7ed4b16c-439e-4ad3-bd97-58a0305924e1.jpg?strip=all&format=webp&resize=2360)Peter Thiel in 2019Kiyoshi Ota/Bloomberg via Getty Images

Thiel and Karp had met at Stanford University, where they got along despite their differences. Karp grew up near Philadelphia in a “super-intellectual, very far left” house as a mixed-race child, his mother an African-American artist, his father a Jewish paediatrician. After Stanford he moved to Germany, where he earned a doctorate in philosophy.

Thiel was a conservative who would later become a critical early backer of both Donald Trump and JD Vance. Thiel persuaded Karp to come back from Germany with the offer to lead his fledgling firm. They co-founded Palantir in 2003 and picked up investors, the CIA’s venture capital arm among them.

Advertisement

Amid the ultra-liberal West Coast tech milieu, Palantir was, from the start, an outcast. Where it celebrated its role in protecting the West and killing bad guys, Big Tech rivals sold ads and proudly refused to get into the “business of war”. Karp grew to despise Silicon Valley, which he viewed as an “island” of amorality.

- [**A tech oligarch’s manifesto for changing the world**](https://www.thetimes.com/culture/books/article/technological-republic-power-belief-future-west-alexander-karp-nicholas-zamiska-review-n2tllb72n)

Palantir built two core products. Gotham is a software platform adept at finding needles in haystacks, be they potential terrorists, illegal immigrants or mines on roads in Iraq. Foundry helps organisations such as BP and the NHS sort through their mountains of data to make better business decisions. Underlying them both is Ontology, the system that digests and translates that data.

Karp took Palantir public in 2020. It was a bumpy ride. Wall Street analysts did not like the business model, given its heavy reliance on “forward-deployed engineers” — software developers who are embedded in an organisation to understand the issues and so deploy software within them more effectively. This approach, to critics, made Palantir look like a consultancy — the type of business that commands much lower valuation multiples than pure software companies.

The outbreak of conflicts from the Middle East to Europe, combined with its specialised products nearly a quarter of a century in the making, changed the game for Palantir. Suddenly its systems, which can help pinpoint the location of terrorists or analyse drone data, were in heavy demand.

Advertisement

“This is Palantir’s time,” Karp likes to say. “There is basically no conflict in the world where western allies are involved, where battlefields are involved, and the stakes are life and death, where Palantir is not the first call.”

- [**How Silicon Valley learned to stop worrying and love the Pentagon**](https://www.thetimes.com/us/business-us/article/silicon-valley-big-tech-ai-donald-trump-google-meta-08qnrvbmf)

He admits that, on Wall Street, his constant crowing has earned him the image of being an “arrogant prick”. His earnings calls can descend into interminable ramblings about Palantir’s “sublime” business, the “thin pagan religion” of wokeness, and the financial pain he has inflicted on those who have bet against Palantir.

“Almost nothing makes a human happier,” he said, “than taking the lines of cocaine away from these short sellers.”

## The UK connection

Britain is Palantir’s largest outpost outside America. The company operates a large office in Soho, central London, and one in five of its workers live on these shores. To the degree that Palantir is known at all by the broader public, however, that probably traces back to the 2023 award of a seven-year contract worth up to £330 million with NHS England.

Advertisement

The health service chose Palantir’s software to collate patient and hospital data spread across different databases into a single platform, to cut waiting times and increase efficiency. According to NHS data, 123 hospital trusts out of 205 are using Palantir’s federated data platform, and 80 were said to be “reporting benefits” at the end of February.

![British Prime Minister Keir Starmer tours Palantir Technologies headquarters, looking at a laptop with an employee.](https://www.thetimes.com/imageserver/image/abb42565-05ec-41c3-b688-6c00296daa37.jpg?strip=all&format=webp&resize=2360)Sir Keir Starmer tours Palantir’s headquarters in Washington with staff and British military personnel in February last yearCARL COURT/POOL/AFP via Getty Images

Given Karp’s support of Israel’s Gaza crackdown and Palantir’s involvement in Donald Trump’s deportation efforts, public worries about the company’s role inside the [health service](https://www.thetimes.com/uk/healthcare/article/nhs-boss-pushes-ahead-palantir-0hf6fn8dw) surface regularly. Labour MP Emily Darlington last month called for fresh scrutiny of the contract: “It’s a major security risk if you’re working with a company whose chief executive talks about the NHS as a ‘delusion’ and doesn’t share the values of the NHS or Britain in general.”

Yet, the Metropolitan Police thought Palantir’s technology was vital in pushing through efficiencies, given its £125 million funding shortfall and the need to cut 1,150 jobs. But its staff association had expressed concerns about an AI tool developed by the tech giant that led to hundreds of officers being suspended for misconduct — a key public issue following the murder of Sarah Everard by a serving police officer.

Met commissioner Sir Mark Rowley had insisted: “This is the Met using technology, data and stronger legal powers to confront poor behaviour, raise standards and fix our foundations, as our communities would expect.”

![Sir Mark Rowley, Commissioner of Police of the Metropolis, London, stands in a control room with multiple screens displaying surveillance feeds.](https://www.thetimes.com/imageserver/image/b798c9c1-2ebe-4188-afd9-72446ace87b3.jpg?strip=all&format=webp&resize=2360)Sir Mark RowleyJohn Nguyen for the Times

It is unclear what, if any, recourse Palantir may have. What does seem certain, however, is that no controversy will keep Karp, the company’s philosopher king, from preaching his brand of strident pro-West, “anti-woke” doctrine. Last year he co-authored a book called _The Technological Republic_, in which he railed against fellow tech executives who had, until recently, refused to engage with western military or intelligence.

“The Silicon Valley giants that dominate the American economy have made the strategic mistake of casting themselves as existing outside the country in which they were built,” he wrote. “Many of them essentially abandoned any serious attempt to advance society.”

That has begun to shift as companies from Google to Microsoft and OpenAI have jumped into the business of war. Karp is leading from the front — happy to take the arrows.
