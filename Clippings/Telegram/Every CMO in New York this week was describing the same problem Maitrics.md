# Every CMO in New York this week was describing the same problem

![](https://www.maitrics.com/images/team%20branded/john%20gower%201.jpg)

John Gower

November 20, 2026 · 6 min read

A big thank you to Aaron Jackson and the Reuters Events team for having me at Strategic Marketing in New York on Monday. I’ve spoken at this event before, so I know how good the room is. This year was different.

AI was everywhere, as you’d expect. But the theme of the day was measurement. On stage and in the conversations afterwards, some of the biggest marketers in the world kept circling the same problem: decisions are moving faster than the evidence behind them.

## The funnel is dead, and the giants know it

Jane Wakely, who leads consumer and marketing at PepsiCo, opened the day by telling the room the funnel has collapsed. Attention is fragmented, and brands are now marketing to AI agents as well as people. PepsiCo’s answer is to organize around what people do: stream, scroll, search, sense and shop.

She wasn’t alone. Mars runs brand and performance media under one team. Strava said its best partnerships lift every metric at once, rather than sitting in one funnel stage.

When the world’s biggest advertisers stop believing in the funnel, every report built on it is measuring a shape that no longer exists.

## They’re building their own measurement

Then came the part I found most telling.

Unilever has built its own framework that puts buzz, engagement, sentiment, search and spend on one footing. It measures paid, earned and organic together, and tracks visibility in AI answers as a sign of consideration. PepsiCo has built its own identity data, at a scale few companies could match. Mars blends its MMM, its brand tracker and its media data into one budget process, just to decide where the money goes.

That’s three of the largest advertisers on earth, each building its own way to see what’s working. Nobody builds that in-house if they can buy it off the shelf.

## The CFO was in every session

Every panel came back to finance. Unilever has data showing that pulling Vaseline advertising in its peak winter quarter does real damage. Mondelēz described past cuts that took months to recover from. One panel offered the test that should hang over every budget meeting: whether today’s decision makes next year’s sales easier or harder.

The CMOs winning budget are winning by speaking finance’s language, not by arguing harder for brand. Sephora tied its AI personalization to incremental revenue and lifetime value. One CMO on the lean marketing panel turned company profit targets into a single customer-acquisition number, and finance found more money and more measurement support.

Everyone else is squeezed. Gartner has marketing budgets flat at 7.8% of revenue, and 56% of CMOs at billion-dollar companies say they can’t fund this year’s plan ( [Gartner](https://gartner.com/en/newsroom/press-releases/2026-05-11-gartner-2026-cmo-spend-survey-finds-cmos-allocate-15-point-3-percent-of-marketing-budgets-to-ai-but-only-30-percent-are-ready-to-scale-ai-capabilities)). The CMO Survey found 70.6% of marketers under pressure now put short-term impact first ( [eMarketer, citing The CMO Survey 2026](https://www.emarketer.com/content/faq-on-marketing-budgets--growth-benchmarks--digital-allocation--brand-performance-imbalance)).

That’s the trap. Brand turns up to the budget meeting with survey scores, and performance turns up with revenue. Revenue wins every time, whatever Binet and Field taught us about the long term.

## The best work is the hardest to measure

The irony of the day went largely unsaid.

Mondelēz let creators talk about Nutter Butter, a brand that had been quiet for years, with no paid media behind it. Sales rose 20%, and the brand picked up more than a million TikTok followers. Unilever asked communities how they used Vaseline, verified those uses in the lab, and reached the brand’s highest-ever market share without a paid campaign. Mars named creator and influencer work among its highest-ROI activity.

The work producing the biggest results is the work traditional measurement struggles to see. There’s no click path, no campaign flight, and often no paid media at all. And because creator content, reviews and Reddit threads feed what AI assistants say about a brand, that work is shaping the shortlist before a shopper ever reaches your site.

## AI makes it faster, not clearer

Heineken has cut creative development from weeks to hours. PepsiCo described AI moving from efficiency to effectiveness, and eventually removing the handoffs between insights, marketing and media altogether.

That’s real progress. It also means brands are about to produce far more marketing, far faster, and most will judge it with measurement that already couldn’t keep up.

The smart brands drew a line. Heineken won’t use AI to show people, bars or moments that don’t exist. Mars told the story of dropping its Dolmio puppets, until a new CMO brought them back because consumers missed them. When AI can make anything, distinctive brand codes become the asset nobody can copy.

## What this means if you’re a challenger

For years, the big brands’ advantage was simple: they could see what everyone else couldn’t. On Monday their CMOs described the same blind spot as everyone else, and showed how much they’re spending to fix it.

PepsiCo, Unilever and Mars can build their own measurement. A $150M challenger with one analyst stretched across everything can’t, and for the most part, that brand is guessing.

The good news is that behavior leaves a trail: searches, site visits, social conversation, competitors’ media spend, what AI assistants recommend and what scans at the till. Your competitors show up in all of it.

If I were running brand at a challenger today, I’d do four things:

1. **Bring revenue to the brand conversation.** Sephora showed how. Reach and awareness won’t win the budget meeting.
2. **Stop letting a twice-a-year MMM run a monthly business.** Keep it, and fill the months between.
3. **Benchmark against competitors, not last year.** Beating last year while your nearest rival pulls ahead is still losing.
4. **Measure creators, earned and AI visibility alongside paid.** That’s where Monday’s biggest results came from.

The giants are spending millions to see what’s working. The challengers who can see it too, without the millions, will take share from brands ten times their size.
