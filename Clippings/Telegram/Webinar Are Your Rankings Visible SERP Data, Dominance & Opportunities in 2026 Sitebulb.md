In summary, rank position alone is no longer a reliable metric for SEO success because most search results now appear below the fold and are obscured by AI Overviews, paid results, and other SERP features, requiring SEO professionals to shift focus toward visibility, impressions, and brand presence instead of clicks.

## Headlines

- **Rank position has become an obsolete SEO metric as most organic results sit below the fold.** The median number-one desktop result appears approximately 1,000 pixels down the page, with more than half of all position-one results now below the typical 800-pixel laptop viewport, requiring five screens of scrolling to reach traditional organic results.

- **AI Overviews, paid results, and product features are systematically pushing organic content further down search results.** Since Google I/O 2024, paid results have grown visually larger and AI Overviews have become increasingly prevalent, with paid results claiming approximately one-third of above-the-fold pixels even on informational queries.

- **Visual prominence and result size now matter more than ranking position for driving impressions and brand visibility.** Organic results enhanced with rich snippets, sitelinks, and video attachments can be one-third larger than standard results, making them more likely to generate impressions and brand recognition regardless of rank position.

- **SEO strategy must pivot from clicks to impressions as the primary success metric.** With fewer organic clicks available due to AI Overviews and other features answering user queries directly, SEO teams should adopt the top-of-funnel marketing mindset of billboard and TV advertising teams, measuring success through visibility and brand presence rather than attributable clicks.

- **Different search verticals show dramatically different levels of organic visibility, requiring keyword-specific strategies.** Home and garden queries show median position-one results three screens down due to product features, while retail queries position organic higher, and different features (Local Packs in dining, Knowledge Graphs in arts and entertainment) present varied opportunities depending on the industry.

Rank position has always been a proxy, a way of estimating how likely a result is to get clicked. But position doesn't tell you whether your result is actually visible on screen, how much space it takes up, or how far a user has to scroll before they even reach it. Those things matter at least as much as the number.

[Tom Capper](https://www.linkedin.com/in/thcapper/), who leads the Search Science team at Moz, has been looking at this through a different lens: visual SERP data.

He joined us to share what that data shows, which result types dominate visually, which verticals are most affected, and where the opportunities are that rank tracking alone won't surface. He also looks at how this framing gives you a more honest way to view paid and AI search results alongside organic.
