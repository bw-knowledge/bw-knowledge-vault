## Carlsberg Reveals 25% Uplift in Ad Attention as New Research Reshapes Traditional Media Planning

Carlsberg Group has released The Carlsberg Effect, a new report developed with dentsu and Lumen Research, revealing how brand distinctiveness is changing the way advertising effectiveness is measured and media investment decisions are made.

The eye-tracking research challenges the assumption that more advertising attention always delivers better results, finding that mature, highly recognisable brands can require less attention to drive recall and choice than less distinctive competitors.

For established brands like Carlsberg, whose iconic green-and-white identity and 'Probably the best beer in the world' tagline have built recognition over decades, this suggests there is no universal attention target for advertisers. How much attention a brand needs to influence consumers depends, in part, on how distinctive and recognisable it already is.

The findings also reinforce the importance of consistent distinctive brand assets, early brand recognition and creative designed to capture attention across different platforms.

The programme is now putting these findings into practice, using brand-specific attention benchmarks alongside traditional media metrics to inform planning, buying and campaign optimisation.

**Key findings from The Carlsberg Effect**

- **Up to 11x more viewed time for the same cost**, achievable through attention-led optimisation in certain cases
- **Up to 21% uplift in brand choice** in tested formats such as social swiping video, against a 5% industry benchmark, with Carlsberg advertising consistently outperforming Lumen's industry norms
- **Distinctive brands need less attention** to drive recall and brand choice, challenging the assumption that more exposure always means better results
- **Attention performance varies by platform**, with Carlsberg's research demonstrating why a one-size-fits-all approach to media planning is ineffective.

**Turning attention into smarter media investment**

Rather than treating every advertising impression equally, Carlsberg is using brand-specific attention data to identify which media placements are most likely to deliver results.

The approach allows the Group to combine higher- and lower-attention media, prioritising cost-effective placements when less attention is sufficient, while investing more where additional attention is likely to generate additional impact.

Lumen’s Attention Measurement Platform (LAMP) has been rolled out across more than 40 markets, enabling Carlsberg to monitor attention and optimise campaigns based on what consumers are likely to see, rather than what is simply served.

**Art Valkenburg, Group Vice President of Media & Digital Transformation at Carlsberg Group, said:**

“In advertising, we’ve become very good at measuring how many impressions we buy and what they cost, but that doesn’t tell us whether we’re making an impact. What this research shows is that brands can turn attention into results very differently. For a brand as recognisable as Carlsberg, it’s not always about more attention; it’s about the right attention, backed by creativity that makes those moments count. For us, that means investing where attention genuinely adds value, and bringing our media and creative teams closer together.”

**From attention measurement to media effectiveness**

Following a foundational year of learning in 2025, Carlsberg is now focused on embedding attention-led planning and optimisation more deeply across its global markets.

Working with strategic partners including Google and Meta, the Group is exploring how AI, creative quality and attention measurement can improve media effectiveness, alongside further testing to establish the impact on wider business outcomes.

Carlsberg-specific attention models, developed with dentsu and Lumen, now inform channel and format choices and campaign optimisation. They’re also bringing media and creative planning closer together, with greater emphasis on early brand recognition and distinctive brand assets.

In keeping with Carlsberg's longstanding commitment to supporting research and sharing knowledge, the Group is making the full report publicly available, giving marketers access to the findings and an opportunity to consider how attention measurement could inform their own media strategies.
